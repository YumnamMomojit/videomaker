Retrieval-Augmented Generation
RAG
What it is — and how it is used in finance
01 · The problem

Large language models are powerful —
but on their own, they fall short

Knowledge cut-off

They only know their training data. A rate change or a circular issued last week is invisible to them.

Hallucinations

When unsure, they can give confident but wrong answers — unacceptable where money and regulation are involved.

No access to your data

Internal policies, loan files and reports were never part of their training, so they cannot answer about them.

02 · What is RAG?

RAG lets an AI model look things up
in trusted documents before it answers

R

Retrieve

Search a trusted knowledge base for the passages most relevant to the question.

A

Augment

Add those passages to the prompt, so the model has the facts in front of it.

G

Generate

The language model writes an answer grounded in that context — and cites its sources.

Think of it as an open-book exam: instead of answering from memory, the model consults the right pages — and shows which pages it used.

03 · How RAG works

Two stages: prepare the knowledge, then answer

1 Indexing · done ahead of time

Documents

Reports, policies, circulars, contracts

Chunk & embed

Split into passages; turn each into a vector of numbers

Vector database

Stores passages so they can be searched by meaning

When documents change, just re-index them — the LLM itself never needs retraining.

2 Answering · at question time

User question

“What is the loan limit for large home loans?”

Embed question

Same embedding model turns it into a vector

Retrieve

Fetch the top-k passages closest in meaning

LLM

Prompt = question + retrieved passages

Grounded answer

With citations to the source passages

1

Indexing: documents are split into chunks and converted into embeddings — vectors of numbers that capture meaning — then stored in a vector database.

2

Retrieval: the question is embedded the same way, and the passages whose vectors are most similar to it are fetched.

3

Generation: the LLM receives the question plus the retrieved passages, and writes an answer that points back to its sources.

04 · Why finance needs RAG

Finance runs on documents, rules and numbers
that keep changing

Information changes daily

Interest rates, regulations, market news and product terms are updated constantly.

Answers must be traceable

Regulators and auditors expect every answer to point back to a source.

Data is highly sensitive

Customer and transaction data must stay inside the institution's controlled systems.

Volumes are enormous

Annual reports, filings, contracts and policies run to thousands of pages.

→ RAG gives fresh, sourced answers from the institution's own documents — without retraining the model.

08 · Getting it right

The benefits — and what to watch out for

Benefits

Up-to-date answers — update the documents, not the model

Traceable — every answer links to its source passages

Private data stays private — protected by the institution's access controls

Watch-outs

Answers are only as good as retrieval — measure and test it

Enforce permissions — users see only documents they are allowed to

Keep a human in the loop for high-stakes financial decisions
